Reston is a planned community, not its own municipality.

Reston was developed as a planned community with its own distinct design, but it does not have a separate town charter or its own municipal government. It is an unincorporated part of Fairfax County, so governance runs through the county rather than a Reston-specific town office. That does not change how we cover the area: the 20190 and 20191 ZIPs are inside our service range, and calls from Reston get the same response as any incorporated city on our list.

We are based near Fairfax, not in Reston itself, so there is no local office or standing crew stationed here. What we offer is a phone line that answers calls from these ZIPs and technicians who route out to handle them.

What we handle on calls from Reston.

Reston's planned layout mixes townhomes, condominiums and single-family homes with extensive shared green space and walking paths, and that combination shapes the calls we get. Properties near a pond, wooded path or shared common area tend to see more outdoor rodent activity than a more isolated lot would.

Indoors, calls commonly describe mice moving into a garage, townhome crawl space or shared building utility area as the weather turns cold. Our mouse control service covers the trap-and-seal approach for that indoor pattern, while rat control addresses outdoor activity near ponds, paths and common green space.

Shared green space and walking paths.

Reston's extensive network of ponds, wooded paths and common areas gives rodents more connected natural cover than a typical subdivision without that shared design. Our backyard and outdoor rodent control service addresses burrows and outdoor nests on individual properties bordering this shared space.

Townhomes and shared walls.

Reston's mix of townhomes and condominiums means rodent activity can sometimes originate in a shared wall, attic or utility chase rather than being isolated to one unit. Our rodent exclusion service seals entry points at the unit level, and we will tell you honestly if an inspection points to a shared-structure issue worth raising with a homeowners association.

Scheduling a visit in a planned community.

Reston's planned design means many properties are managed under a homeowners association with its own rules about contractor access, parking and scheduling. We work within those rules when they apply, and it helps to mention on the call whether your unit is part of an association with a specific check-in procedure. Beyond that, scheduling works the same way as anywhere else in our coverage area: a phone call, a description of what you are seeing, and a visit time that fits your schedule.

When to call it an emergency.

Active chewing near wiring, a nest close to a heat source, or an animal loose inside living space are reasons to flag a call as urgent. The line is open 24/7, and we prioritize these situations against the regular schedule.

Reston's distance from our Fairfax base is short enough that same-day scheduling is realistic on most days, whether the visit covers a townhome, a condo unit or a single-family lot bordering the community's shared green space. Confirming your unit type ahead of time helps us bring the right approach from the start.

What to expect on the call.

  • Tell us your ZIP code and general location so we can route the visit correctly.
  • Describe what you saw or heard, and roughly when it started.

Before we arrive.

  • Keep pets and children away from any suspected nest or burrow.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings.
  • Do not set out loose bait or poison before we arrive.
  • Note where and when you saw or heard activity.
  • Clear access to the crawl space, garage or utility area if you can do so safely.
