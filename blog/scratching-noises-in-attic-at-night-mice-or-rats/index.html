Scratching Noises in the Attic at Night: Mice or Rats?

The sound, timing and location of attic noise all give clues about what is actually up there, and getting it right saves time on the fix.

What the timing tells you.

Mice and rats are both nocturnal, so activity that picks up after dark and continues through the night is consistent with either. Squirrels, on the other hand, are active during the day, so scratching that happens mostly in daylight hours points away from a rodent problem and toward a squirrel instead. Bats can also produce noise in an attic, typically around dusk and dawn as they leave and return, which is a different pattern from the more continuous, scattered noise rodents make.

If the noise happens consistently after dark and continues intermittently through the night, mice or rats are the more likely explanation, and the next clues come from the sound itself and where it seems to originate.

What the sound itself suggests.

Mice tend to produce lighter, quicker scratching and scurrying sounds, sometimes accompanied by faint squeaking. Rats produce a heavier, more deliberate scratching or thumping sound, since their larger body weight is more noticeable moving across insulation or along a joist. Gnawing sounds, a more rhythmic chewing noise rather than scratching, can come from either animal working on wood, wiring insulation or a stubborn entry point.

If you can pinpoint roughly where the sound comes from, that helps too. Noise near the eaves or roofline more often suggests an animal that just entered, while noise concentrated in one spot over several nights suggests an established nest.

Why location in the attic matters.

An older Fairfax home near the Historic District with mature trees nearby is more likely to have roof rat activity entering near the roofline, given their climbing habits. Mice more often enter through eave gaps, vent screens or a chimney chase and then spread out across the attic looking for nesting material. Noting whether the noise seems to travel across the attic or stay in one general area over multiple nights gives a useful clue about how established the activity is.

Confirming with visual signs.

If it is safe to check, look for droppings, chewed insulation, or shredded material that indicates nesting. Mouse droppings are small and pointed. Rat droppings are larger, either blunt-ended for Norway rats or spindle-shaped for roof rats. Fresh, dark droppings suggest recent activity, while dry, faded ones suggest the activity may be older.

Do not disturb any droppings you find without ventilating the space first and wetting the material with disinfectant, following CDC guidance for safe cleanup.

When the noise is worth calling about immediately.

If the scratching seems to be moving toward a living space, such as through a ceiling into a bedroom, or if you hear what sounds like chewing near what could be electrical wiring, treat it as more urgent than routine attic noise. Our line is open 24/7 for exactly this kind of situation.

A note on squirrels and bats.

If the noise pattern points toward a squirrel or bat rather than a rodent, that changes the response, since squirrels are typically handled through wildlife removal services and bats require specific protected-species handling under state and federal rules. We can tell you honestly if an inspection suggests one of these rather than assuming every attic noise is automatically a rodent.

Why the same noise sounds louder in some rooms.

A bedroom directly under the section of attic where activity is concentrated will pick up more sound than a room at the opposite end of the house, simply because of distance and the path sound travels through joists and insulation. A homeowner sometimes assumes activity has spread across the whole attic when in reality it may be confined to one area, and the noise is simply carrying more clearly into whichever room happens to sit closest to that spot. This is worth mentioning when you call, since knowing which room hears the noise most clearly gives a useful starting point for where to check first.

When multiple people in the house hear different things.

It is not unusual for one person in a household to describe scratching while another describes more of a thumping or rolling sound, even when both are hearing the same underlying activity. Sound perception varies by hearing sensitivity, the exact time each person happened to be listening, and even which side of a shared wall a bedroom sits on. Rather than trying to reconcile conflicting descriptions into one single answer before calling, it helps simply to share both descriptions, since a technician can often make more sense of the variation than either description alone would suggest.

What an inspection reveals that guessing cannot.

Sound and timing narrow down the likely animal, but only a direct inspection confirms species, location and the extent of activity with certainty. A homeowner who guesses correctly still benefits from having that guess confirmed before treatment starts, since acting on an incorrect guess, treating for mice when roof rats are actually present, for example, wastes time and can delay addressing the real entry point. We would rather confirm the details directly than have you commit to a plan based on an educated guess alone, however reasonable that guess might be.

Before you call about attic noise.

  • Note whether the noise happens mainly at night, at dawn and dusk, or during the day.
  • Listen for whether the sound stays in one spot or seems to move around.
  • Do not disturb any droppings or nesting material you see without proper precautions.
  • Mention any nearby mature trees if you suspect the noise is coming from near the roofline.

Common questions

Could the noise just be branches on the roof?

It is possible, especially on windy nights, but consistent scratching that continues on calm nights too is more likely an animal than branches.

Is it dangerous to go into the attic myself to check?

It is generally safe to look with a flashlight from the attic access point, but avoid disturbing droppings or nesting material directly, and do not attempt to catch or handle any animal you see.

Why does the noise seem worse some nights than others?

Rodent activity can vary with temperature, recent weather and how much food is available elsewhere, so some nights show more movement than others even with an ongoing problem.

Can squirrels and mice be in the same attic at once?

It is possible but less common, since the two often compete for similar spaces. An inspection can confirm which animal, or animals, are actually present.

How fast can I get someone out to check?

Same-day visits happen when the schedule allows. Call our 24/7 line and describe what you are hearing.
