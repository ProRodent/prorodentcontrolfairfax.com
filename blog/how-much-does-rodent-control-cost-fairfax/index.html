The honest answer is that cost depends on several variables specific to your property, and any number given without an inspection is a guess rather than a quote.

Why we do not quote a flat price over the phone.

Rodent control cost depends heavily on factors that only become clear during an inspection: how many entry points a house has, whether the problem is mice or rats or both, how extensive any existing damage is, and how many structures on the property need attention. A quote given without seeing the property risks being either misleadingly low or unnecessarily high, so we provide an estimate after a technician has actually looked at your specific situation.

Our rodent inspection and estimate service exists specifically for this reason, giving you a clear number based on what your home actually needs rather than a generic average.

Why an older Old Town home might cost differently than newer construction.

An older home near Fairfax's Historic District might have more numerous, smaller settled gaps at sills, chimneys and window frames, developed over decades. A newer home closer to the city's edges might instead have fewer but sometimes larger gaps at utility penetrations left from construction. Neither pattern is automatically more or less expensive, since cost tracks the actual number and size of gaps found, not simply the age of the house.

Why rats can cost differently than mice.

A rat problem, especially one involving outdoor burrows along a fence line or creek bank, sometimes covers more ground than a typical indoor mouse problem. The inspection and treatment need to address the full extent of a burrow network rather than just entry points into the house itself. Our rat control service accounts for this broader scope when the situation calls for it.

How the number of visits affects total cost.

A single mouse that wandered in once and was caught on the first visit costs less overall than an established infestation that needs several checks before catches taper off, simply because more visits mean more time and materials. This is one reason two households with what sounds like a similar problem over the phone can end up with different total costs once the inspection reveals how established each situation actually is. We would rather explain this variability honestly upfront than let a caller assume every job costs roughly the same amount regardless of severity, since that assumption tends to lead to disappointment once the actual inspection findings come back.

Larger lots and outdoor-heavy jobs.

Properties with a larger lot, multiple outbuildings or a section bordering wooded or drainage-adjacent land, patterns common in neighborhoods like Fairfax Farms or Pine Ridge, tend to need a more extensive outdoor inspection than a smaller, more open lot closer to the city center. That additional ground to cover factors into the estimate the same way additional entry points on the house itself would, since a technician needs time to walk and assess the full property rather than just the structure closest to the street.

Comparing an estimate to a lower quote elsewhere.

If you have received a lower estimate from another company, it is worth asking what that estimate actually includes, since a lower number sometimes reflects a narrower scope, such as trapping alone without sealing or a follow-up visit. We would rather walk you through exactly what our estimate covers so you can compare it fairly against another company's scope, rather than assuming two different numbers are quotes for the identical job. That comparison is more useful to you than the raw number alone, especially once repair and follow-up visits are factored into the total cost of getting the job fully finished rather than just started.

The order of services and why it matters for total cost.

Skipping steps to save money upfront often costs more over time. Sealing without first removing active rodents can trap animals inside a wall, creating an odor problem that needs separate treatment. Cleaning up before treatment is confirmed complete means redoing the cleanup once new droppings appear. Following the proper order, inspect, treat, seal, follow up, generally costs less overall than jumping between steps out of order.

What a follow-up visit typically involves.

Most rodent control plans include at least one follow-up visit to confirm traps and stations are no longer catching anything and that sealed areas are holding. This follow-up is usually included in the original estimate rather than billed as a separate surprise cost, though the specifics depend on the scope of the original job.

Repair costs, separate from removal and sealing.

If chewed insulation, ductwork or drywall needs repair, that cost is separate from removal and sealing, since our rodent damage repair service only starts once the space is confirmed clear and sealed. Getting an estimate for both the removal and sealing work, plus any needed repair, gives you the full picture rather than a partial number.

Commercial properties and cost.

Businesses often see different cost factors than homes, since ongoing monitoring, documentation for health inspections, and coordination around operating hours all add to the scope of a commercial job. Our commercial rodent control service estimates these jobs separately from residential work given the different considerations involved.

Getting an accurate estimate for your property.

The most reliable way to know what your specific situation will cost is to call and describe what you are experiencing, then schedule an inspection. We explain the estimate clearly before any work begins, so you can decide whether to move forward with full information rather than a rough guess.

What to have ready for an accurate estimate.

  • A description of where and when you have noticed activity.
  • Whether you suspect mice, rats, or are not sure which.
  • Any outbuildings, sheds or additional structures on the property.
  • Whether you have noticed any existing damage, such as chewed insulation or wiring.

Common questions

Why can't you just give me a ballpark number now?

A ballpark number without seeing the property risks being inaccurate in either direction. An inspection lets us give you a real estimate based on what your home actually needs.

Is there a cost just for the inspection visit?

We do not publish pricing here, since terms can change, but call and we will explain current inspection terms clearly before scheduling.

Does a bigger house always cost more to treat?

Not necessarily. Cost tracks the number of entry points and the extent of activity more directly than square footage alone.

Will you tell me the estimate before starting any work?

Yes, we explain the estimate clearly after the inspection and before any treatment or sealing begins, so you can decide whether to proceed.

Does an outdoor rat problem cost more than an indoor mouse problem?

It can, if the burrow network covers significant ground, since the inspection and treatment scope grows with the size of the area involved.
