Fort Myer is a military installation, not a civilian town.

Fort Myer is part of Joint Base Myer-Henderson Hall, a U.S. Army installation, and it is not a civilian residential town, unincorporated community or independent city the way most other places on our coverage list are. We frame this honestly: calls connected to this ZIP are handled the same way calls near any other military-adjacent installation in our broader service network are, respecting the base's own security and access procedures rather than treating it as an ordinary residential community.

We are based near Fairfax, not on Fort Myer itself, so there is no local office or standing crew stationed here. What we offer is a phone line that answers calls connected to this ZIP and technicians who coordinate any necessary access with the appropriate base contacts.

What we handle on calls connected to Fort Myer.

Housing and facilities connected to a military installation face many of the same rodent pressures as civilian properties nearby. Mice seek shelter in garages or crawl spaces, rats travel along fence lines or near dumpster areas, and the same seasonal patterns affect any property in the region. The difference is mainly procedural, involving base access and coordination rather than a different set of rodent behaviors.

Our mouse control and rat control services apply the same inspection-first approach used across our civilian coverage area, adjusted for any access requirements a specific facility or housing unit requires.

Planning enough lead time for access coordination.

Because a visit connected to Fort Myer typically involves an extra coordination step that a civilian address does not, calling with as much lead time as the situation allows helps avoid delays. For a routine inspection, that might mean a day or two to confirm access. For an urgent situation, we will work to coordinate access as quickly as the base's own procedures allow, but the timeline is not entirely within our control the way it is for a civilian residence.

When to call it an emergency.

Active chewing near wiring, a nest close to a heat source, or an animal loose inside living space are reasons to flag a call as urgent, the same as anywhere else in our coverage area. The line is open 24/7, and we will discuss access coordination as part of scheduling an urgent visit.

Fort Myer's distance from our Fairfax base is short enough that travel time itself is rarely the limiting factor. Access coordination, not distance, is what most often determines how quickly a visit can actually happen here.
