How rodent control works in Fairfax

Most calls we take here fall into a few patterns. Scratching in a crawl space or attic at night. Droppings in a garage or pantry. A dead-animal smell that will not fade. Chewed wires in a car that has been parked for a few days. Burrow holes along a fence line or near a shed. Each one points to a different fix.

Mice and rats need different handling. Mice are small, breed all year indoors and slip through gaps near 1/4 inch, so they favor attics, garages and pantries. Rats are larger, burrow, and follow water and food waste along fence lines, storm drains and creek banks. Our mouse control and rat control visits start from that split.

Fairfax's climate makes the split matter more than it would somewhere with a short winter. Northern Virginia gets a cool, damp fall and a winter with real cold snaps, and both push rodents toward shelter for months at a time. Homes near Accotink Creek and Daniels Run see more rat activity along foundations and fence lines, while homes on higher, older streets near the Historic District see more mice moving into attics and garages as temperatures drop.

The service follows one order. We inspect to find the entry points and the species, treat the active animals, seal the gaps, and follow up to confirm the activity has stopped. A rodent inspection can be the first step when you do not know where they get in.

Trapping alone rarely ends a problem, because new animals move in through the same gaps. That is why rodent exclusion is part of the plan. If the noise is a night-time emergency, calling the line directly is the fastest route.

Our four-step approach

  1. Inspect

    We walk the exterior and interior, checking rooflines, vent screens, crawl space openings and foundation edges. On older homes near Old Town we look first at the sills and chimney flashing, where settling opens small gaps over time.

  2. Treat

    We remove active animals with traps and tamper-resistant stations placed out of reach of children and pets. We avoid loose bait, and we explain what is placed and where before we leave.

  3. Seal

    We close entry points with materials rodents cannot chew through, such as hardware cloth, sheet metal and mortar. Foam alone does not last through a Virginia winter freeze-thaw cycle. Crawl space vents and older brick foundations are common fixes on Fairfax homes.

  4. Follow up

    We return to check traps and stations and confirm the noise, droppings and gnawing have stopped. If activity continues, we look for the gap we missed instead of simply adding more traps.

Signs you have mice or rats

Rodents leave clues long before you see one. These six are the ones we hear about most.

  • Droppings

    Mouse droppings are about 1/8 to 1/4 inch and pointed. Rat droppings run roughly 1/2 to 3/4 inch and blunt. Do not sweep them dry.

  • Gnaw marks

    Fresh chew marks on wood, plastic, wiring or food packaging look pale and rough. Chewed wiring can contribute to fire risk.

  • Night noises

    Scratching, scurrying or squeaking in walls, ceilings or crawl spaces after dark usually means active nesting close by.

  • Nests

    Shredded paper, fabric and insulation packed into corners, boxes or engine bays point to a nest and a breeding site.

  • Grease marks

    Dark, oily smears along baseboards and pipes show a runway that rodents use again and again.

  • Odors

    A musty ammonia smell suggests heavy activity. A sharp rotting smell in a wall or crawl space often means a dead animal.

Why rodents find Fairfax homes

  • City of Fairfax Historic District

    The historic core around Old Town has some of the city's oldest housing stock, with mature trees and settled framing. Older sills, chimneys and foundation edges in this district are exactly the kind of small, aged gaps we check first on nearby calls.

  • Old Town Hall (1900)

    The 1900 building anchors a stretch of downtown with older construction on tight lots and mature landscaping. Homes and small businesses near it share the same aged-gap pattern common across Old Town.

  • George Mason University

    The university sits just outside the city limits, adjacent to Fairfax rather than inside it, and the streets and rental housing near that edge see steady turnover, seasonal vacancy and stored belongings that can draw mice looking for undisturbed shelter.

Fairfax is a small independent city, not simply a neighborhood of surrounding Fairfax County, and its layout shapes where activity concentrates. Older streets near the Historic District and Truro Church have settled framing, aged chimneys and sills that open small gaps over decades. Newer construction on the outer edges of the city can still have unsealed utility penetrations and vent gaps that a mouse fits through easily, since a mouse needs only about 1/4 inch.

Lower ground near Accotink Creek and Daniels Run holds more moisture, and rats follow that water and any nearby food source along drainage ditches and fence lines toward the nearest foundation. Crawl space construction, common across the city, gives rodents a dry, enclosed space right under the living area if vent screens or access doors are not sealed well.

Northern Virginia's cool fall and cold winter push rodents toward shelter earlier than a milder climate would, and they stay active indoors for a longer stretch once they find a way in. Checking the crawl space access point is one of the first things we do on a call here.

Rodent control questions from Fairfax homeowners

Do you respond at night or on weekends?

Yes. Someone answers around the clock, and same-day visits are offered when bookings permit. If animals are active in your living space at night, call right away.

How do I tell mice from rats?

Check droppings and gaps. Mouse droppings are small and pointed, and mice fit through 1/4 inch openings. Rat droppings are larger and blunt, and rats often burrow outside near foundations, sheds and water.

Why do traps alone fail?

Traps remove the animals you have today but do not close the gaps. New rodents follow scent trails in through the same openings. Sealing entry points is what keeps the problem from returning.

Is treatment safe for pets and children?

We use tamper-resistant bait stations and place them out of reach of children, pets and wildlife. Tell us about pets and where they roam, and we will choose placements around them.

What should I have ready when I call?

Your street, where you hear or see activity, when it started, and whether you have seen droppings, chewing or dead animals. Mention pets, children and whether your home has a crawl space.

Do you handle commercial properties?

Yes. Shops, offices and restaurants near Old Town and along the main commercial corridors face the same pressures as homes. Our commercial rodent control service covers them. Call and describe the building.

Is George Mason University inside the City of Fairfax?

No, the campus sits adjacent to the city rather than inside its boundary, though we cover addresses near that edge the same as anywhere else in our coverage ZIPs.
